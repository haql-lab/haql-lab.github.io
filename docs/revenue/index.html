What haql-revenue does

haql-revenue compares production, lease records, purchaser statements and optional run tickets or contracts. It looks for missing statements, price and volume variances, unscheduled deductions, duplicate lines and value that does not add up.

In one sentence: It turns “the statement looks about right” into a dollar-ranked exception list.

Why it matters

Differences that look like rounding on one lease become real money across hundreds of wells and several purchasers. A revenue accountant does not have time to re-derive every lease, so the check becomes a spot check that never reaches the quiet shortfall.

  • Production can be booked with no statement line at all.
  • A stale price or keying error can repeat every month it goes unnoticed.
  • Paid volume can fall below the produced and ticketed volume.
  • Deductions can exceed the contract, or a line can be paid twice.
Without haql-revenueThe hidden problem

One purchaser is spot-checked and the rest are paid as received. Differences under a thousand dollars per lease stay invisible against the month's gross.

With haql-revenueThe visible answer

Every lease, product and month is reconciled; exceptions are ordered by dollars and arrive with the statement line and source file behind them.

What it checks

The checks cover the full statement chain, from missing lines to value arithmetic.

Check areaWhat it flags
Missing statement or productionA booked volume with no statement line, or a paid line with no production behind it.
Volume variance and zero volumePaid volume differs from produced volume beyond tolerance, or production pays zero.
Price varianceStatement price differs from the fixed contract price.
Unscheduled deductionDeductions exceed the allowed contract rate.
Value mismatchNet value does not equal volume times price minus deductions.
Duplicate and negative linesIdentical lines for one lease/product/month, or an unexplained negative adjustment.
Unmapped lease and unit mismatchA purchaser name with no alias, or units that cannot be converted.
Run-ticket checksA missing ticket or a ticket volume that disagrees with production.

Severity follows dollar impact: high at $10,000 or more, medium at $1,000, low below that. Thresholds are configurable, and findings are advisory questions with evidence.

What it does not do

A useful tool should be clear about its boundaries. These are the current limits, not hidden caveats.

  • No PDF or scanned statement extraction yet; the CSVs must come from an export or data file.
  • Index-based formulas such as West Texas Intermediate (WTI) plus a differential, or a gas basis, are not applied in this version.
  • Multiple purchasers on one lease are aggregated; price and deduction checks are skipped with a warning.
  • No gas-plant or natural gas liquids (NGL) settlement, prior-period accruals, recoupment, severance tax or net revenue interest.
  • No enterprise resource planning (ERP), accounting or supervisory control and data acquisition (SCADA) connector; the bundled review UI is a local pilot surface, not a hosted multi-tenant app.

Findings are advisory. The tool names a contradiction or a risk with the evidence behind it. The engineering, legal or commercial judgement remains with you.

Common questions

Does it read PDF statements?

Not yet. The current version works from CSV exports and data files.

Does it decide whether a deduction is legal?

No. It flags a variance against the supplied contract and evidence; the commercial or legal judgement remains with you.

Can it handle multiple purchasers on one lease?

It aggregates them and skips price and deduction checks for that key with a warning. Per-purchaser allocation is a future feature.

What is a purchaser statement?

The monthly statement from the company that buys the oil or gas. It records volumes, prices, deductions and net value.
