What haql-rrc does

haql-rrc checks a monthly production or Form PR export against the operator-number, district and disposition-code rules printed for the form. A P-5 number is the operator registration number the form uses to attribute the volume. It names each violation by source row and can show the filing deadline and a fine range based on your own penalty rate.

In one sentence: It turns the district's rejection reasons into a pre-filing checklist.

Why it matters

An RRC reject is not a bug report. It is a late filing, an amended filing and a clock that starts before the correction is ready. The PR form is validated against a printed table; every rule is knowable before submission.

  • A missing P-5 operator number makes the volume unattributable.
  • A code that looks plausible but is not on the printed form is still rejected.
  • A volume with no disposition code cannot be balanced.
  • A district outside the valid range prevents the row from being routed.
Without haql-rrcThe hidden problem

The export is filed as produced. Days later the district rejects it on an operator number or a disposition code. The fix is cheap on Monday and expensive after filing.

With haql-rrcThe visible answer

Every tripped rule is named to the row before submission, with the deadline and a fine range calculated from your own rate.

What it checks

The rules follow the printed Form PR tables and the filing structure.

Check areaWhat it flags
Operator numberA row with no P-5 number, or a file carrying more than one operator number.
Oil and gas codesA disposition code that is not in the printed Form PR tables.
Volume without a codeA disposition volume with no code saying what happened to it.
Flare remarkA flared or vented gas code without the required remark.
DistrictA missing district or a district outside 1–9.
Embedded QCThe same structural and numeric checks used by haql-qc run first.
Deadline and fineThe due date and a user-supplied penalty rate turn findings into a clock and a range.

Fine amounts are never asserted from an internal schedule. The tool quantifies only when you provide --fine-rate.

What it does not do

A useful tool should be clear about its boundaries. These are the current limits, not hidden caveats.

  • It does not submit to RRC eFiling and does not replace the official form.
  • It is specific to Texas RRC Form PR, not a general regulatory engine.
  • It does not provide legal advice or assert a fine amount without your rate.
  • It depends on the export containing the fields the form requires.

Findings are advisory. The tool names a contradiction or a risk with the evidence behind it. The engineering, legal or commercial judgement remains with you.

Common questions

Does it file to the RRC?

No. It checks the export before you file. Submission remains with your existing process.

Are the fines official?

No. A dollar range is produced only from the rate you supply; the tool does not invent a penalty schedule.

Can it check a different state?

The current product is built around Texas RRC Form PR. Other jurisdictions would be a separate rule set.

What is Form PR?

The Texas Railroad Commission's monthly production report, used to report oil, gas and water volumes by lease or well.
