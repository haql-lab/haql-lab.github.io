haql-dataroom · A&D dataroom QC

In an acquisition, the seller's export is the claim. haql-dataroom cross-checks it.

Before a deal closes, the dataroom's production, well master, RRC-reported volumes, revenue statements, severance-tax lines, reserve summary and LAS headers have to agree with each other. They rarely do. haql-dataroom scrubs the package offline, cross-checks every table against every other, and names each contradiction to the row — so you negotiate on facts, not on the seller's spreadsheet.

Header names can be whatever the seller used — the tool maps them to canonical roles by synonym, and recognises the files by filename.

What it is

A folder of seller exports in. A cross-checked red-flag sheet out.

No data-room portal. No upload. Just the files the seller handed over, checked against each other the way a technical reviewer should check them — but exhaustively and reproducibly.

Goes in

Production, well master, RRC volumes, revenue, severance tax and reserve CSVs plus any LAS well-log headers. Column names are mapped to canonical roles by synonym and the files are recognised by filename hint; --map ROLE=PATH overrides the guess. Sensitive, so the whole thing runs on your machine.

Comes out

A dataroom audit (JSON + HTML): a self-contained offline red-flag sheet with one record per cross-table finding, each traced to its source row, plus a machine-checkable JSON for your own models.

Why you need it

The contradictions that move a price are spread across seven files.

None of them is visible in any single table. Each one takes an analyst cross-referencing two exports by hand — which is exactly the work that runs out of time before the bid.

Production that has no well

A well reports volumes in the production file but is absent from the well master. You are paying for barrels with no title record behind them (DR001).

The RRC variance

Internal production disagrees with the RRC-reported lease volume. One of the two is what you will actually be paid on — and a reviewer will ask which (DR003).

Production while abandoned

A well marked plugged or inactive still reports production. Either the status is wrong or the volumes are (DR007).

The unsupported decline

A claimed remaining volume is not supported by the production history it is supposedly derived from (DR014) — the reserve number that carries the most price.

Without haql-dataroom

An analyst opens the folders, spot-checks the production and the reserve summary, and moves on. Cross-table contradictions — the ones that change the number — are found after signing, or not at all.

The deal closes on the seller's arithmetic.

What it actually catches

Fifteen ways a dataroom contradicts itself.

The full check list
  • Orphan production — a producing well absent from the well master (DR001)
  • Barren well — a well in the master with no production history in the package (DR002)
  • RRC variance — internal production disagrees with the RRC-reported lease volume (DR003)
  • Interest identity — a WI / NRI / royalty line that violates the revenue-interest identity (DR004)
  • Malformed API — a well identifier that is not a well-formed Texas API number (DR005)
  • Duplicate well — the same well appearing twice in the master with conflicting data (DR006)
  • Production while abandoned — a plugged or inactive well still reporting production (DR007)
  • Shut-in contradiction — days-on and reported volume that cannot both be true (DR008)
  • Producing before drilling — production dated before the well was completed (DR009)
  • LAS mismatches — a log header absent from the master (DR010), or two headers claiming one well but disagreeing (DR011)
  • Unmatched revenue — a revenue statement referencing a lease or well not in the package (DR012)
  • Severance back-check — severed-taxable volume disagreeing with produced volume (DR013)
  • Unsupported decline — a claimed remaining volume not supported by the history (DR014)
  • Revenue value — a revenue statement that is internally inconsistent (DR015)
  • Embedded QC — the same structural and numeric QC pass as haql-qc runs first, so an unparseable row is caught before the cross-table rules read it

Same design stance as the rest of the suite: read-only, offline, deterministic, traceable to the row — which matters most when the data is a deal in progress.
