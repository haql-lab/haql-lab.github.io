haql-coords · Free library

The well-bore formula is right. Is it the formula you meant?

Copy-pasted trigonometry sits in a hundred spreadsheets, and every one is a potential off-by-one in a well record. haql-coords gives you coordinate distance, offsets and well-bore geometry as checked, round-trippable functions — the same geometry the haql suite relies on. Free, MIT-licensed, no account, no cloud.

offset((5.0, 0), bearing=90, length=5)

Why you need it

The formula you copied is probably fine. Being sure is the point.

The inherited formula

The cell came from the workbook left by someone who left years ago. Nobody can say what datum or convention it assumes.

The off-by-one record

One well's coordinates shifted by a digit and the nearest-well calculation quietly changes. Geometry checks are how that surfaces.

The unverifiable answer

A distance is a number; being able to invert it is a proof. Copied formulas rarely round-trip cleanly, which is exactly the slip you want caught before it matters.

The bug in QC's blind spot

QC rules that compare wells need trustworthy geometry upstream. haql-coords is that upstream, shared across the suite.

How it works

Small functions, checked both ways.

Deliberately small API: distances, offsets, and geometry helpers that return numbers you can reproduce by hand. No dependencies pulling a whole analysis stack behind them.
