What it is

Four CSVs and a config in. A credit schedule and evidence trail out.

No accounting system integration. No database. Just the records you already keep, checked against the physics of the storage site.

Goes in

Capture, injection, monitoring and lab CSVs plus a facility config: storage formation, construction and placed-in-service dates, disposition (storage / utilisation / DAC), and the MRV plan. Missing files are treated as gaps, not guessed around.

Comes out

Credits priced year by year at the statutory rate for the construction year, a Subpart RR due-clock, and a certification pack (JSON + HTML) with the full mass balance, storage report, findings and credit schedule — plus a credits.csv you can audit.

Why you need it

The credit is the easiest part. Proving it is the hard part.

Calculating $85/tonne is a multiplication table. Surviving review — where a reviewer asks exactly what you claim, from exactly which wells, into exactly which formation — is where claims come apart.

The vented tonne you forgot

A monitoring quarter records a leak event. Nobody carries it into the balance. The stored number is overstated by exactly the emissions you recorded — and the reviewer finds it in one line.

The formation mismatch

Injection recorded into a formation that is not the storage formation in your filing. It is either a typo in the export or a fundamental problem with the claim — a reviewer will ask which.

The incomplete year

One monitoring quarter missing. One lab quarter missing. The claim is not wrong — it is incomplete, which is close enough to a rejection for most purposes.

The wrong rate

Construction before the Inflation Reduction Act means an inflation-adjusted rate table — not the full $85. Use the wrong table and the whole schedule restates. Nobody wants that letter.

Without haql-45q

The year is summed in a spreadsheet. Stored = injected − emitted. The reviewer asks for the formation, the quarters, the rate basis. The supporting evidence is scattered across four exports and a folder of emails.

Every answer is a fresh search. Every search is a chance to be wrong.

What it actually catches

Eight ways a claim is weaker than it looks.

The full check list
  • Mass-balance gaps — captured CO2 that did not end up injected or vented, beyond a 5% tolerance (RQ010)
  • Emissions eating the storage claim — vented tonnes that make stored tonnes negative (RQ011)
  • Formation inconsistencies — injection recorded outside the storage formation in the config (RQ101)
  • Depth unreported — injection rows carrying no depth for the storage interval (RQ103)
  • Missing quarters — a monitoring or lab gap that makes the year's claim incomplete before you file (RQ201, RQ203)
  • Credit-window violations — construction after the Dec 31 2032 deadline (RQ231), or reporting outside the 12-year window (RQ232)
  • Rate integrity — pre-IRA construction uses the inflation-adjusted table, never a guessed number (RQ301)
  • The reporting clock — Subpart RR annual report due date computed per 40 CFR §98.167(b), so the deadline is in the output, not in someone's calendar

Same design stance as the rest of the suite: read-only, offline, deterministic, traceable to the row.
